# Model Performance Trade-offs Explained
#############################################

Hello! This document explains the results from our two recent model training runs. 
Each model developed a different "personality" for detecting forgeries, and understanding this difference is key to improving the project.

---
### The Two Models: Our Digital Detectives
---

Think of your two models as two different detectives assigned to the same case.

**1. Model V1: The Eager Detective**
This was your first model after we increased the training data.

* **Key Trait:** High Recall (68%), Low Precision (56%).
* **Behavior:** This detective is eager to catch every possible suspect. 
It successfully finds most of the culprits (it catches 68% of all forgeries). 
However, in its eagerness, it also accuses a lot of innocent people (nearly half its accusations are wrong).
* **In Practice:** This model is good at *finding* forgeries but creates a lot of "false alarms."

**2. Model V2: The Cautious Detective**
This was your second model after we lowered the learning rate and adjusted the weights.

* **Key Trait:** High Precision (65%), Low Recall (52%).
* **Behavior:** This detective is very careful and methodical. 
It will only make an accusation if it's very confident. 
As a result, when it accuses a suspect, it's usually right (65% of its forgery flags are correct). 
However, its cautious nature means it lets a lot of culprits slip away (it misses almost half of the forgeries).
* **In Practice:** This model is more *reliable* when it flags a forgery, but it misses more of them.

---
### Understanding the Key Terms
---

**Precision: The "Quality" of Predictions**
* **Question it answers:** When the model says it found a forgery, how often is it correct?
* **Simple Analogy:** Imagine a smoke alarm. High precision means that when the alarm goes off, there is almost certainly a fire. 
A low-precision alarm might go off every time you make toast.
* **Your Model V2 has higher precision.**

**Recall: The "Quantity" of Detections**
* **Question it answers:** Of all the forgeries that actually exist, what percentage did the model find?
* **Simple Analogy:** For the smoke alarm, high recall means it will detect every single real fire, big or small. 
A low-recall alarm might miss a small, smoldering fire.
* **Your Model V1 has higher recall.**

---
### The Trade-off: Why Can't We Just Have Both?
---

Increasing precision often lowers recall, and vice-versa.

Think of it like setting a "confidence" threshold.

* To get **High Recall**, you set the confidence bar low. The model flags anything that looks even slightly suspicious. 
This means you catch more forgeries, but you also incorrectly flag more innocent, but unusual-looking, digits.
* To get **High Precision**, you set the confidence bar high. The model only flags things it is absolutely sure about. 
This means your flags are very trustworthy, but you miss all the borderline cases that don't meet the high bar.

---
### Your Next Decision: What is the Goal of Your App?
---

The "best" model depends on what you consider a bigger problem:

* **Scenario A: A false alarm is not a big deal.**
    If you want to find as many potential forgeries as possible for a human to review later, then a false alarm is acceptable. 
    The most important thing is not to miss any.
    * **In this case, aim for High Recall (like Model V1).**

* **Scenario B: A false accusation is a big problem.**
    If flagging a digit as a forgery automatically triggers a serious action, you want to be very sure before you do. 
    Missing a few forgeries is better than making a wrong accusation.
    * **In this case, aim for High Precision (like Model V2).**

Your next step in tuning the model is to decide which of these scenarios is closer to your goal and 
adjust the training parameters (like the `loss_weight`) to find the perfect "sweet spot"
 between the Eager Detective and the Cautious Detective.
